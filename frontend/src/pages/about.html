About CivicTrace

CivicTrace is a civic-issue reporting and accountability platform. Citizens report problems such as potholes, garbage, water leaks and broken streetlights with a photo. The city can then see, prioritise, fix and prove the fix, and every step is recorded permanently.

Created and built by Debarshi Patikar.

The problem

Reports vanish

Complaints are made by phone, paper or social media, and nobody can see what happened to them afterwards.

No priority

A tiny crack and a dangerous crater look the same in a queue, so urgent issues wait.

No proof

An issue can be marked "fixed" without anyone checking, and there is no record of who did what.

Privacy fear

People hesitate to report when they must give personal details.

How CivicTrace solves it

Anonymous reporting

Citizens never create an account. They get a private token like CT-AB12-CD34 and a case ID to follow their report.

AI triage

Google Gemini looks at the photo, checks it really shows the reported issue, and scores how severe it is.

Hotspot ranking

Many open reports of the same kind in one small area raise that area's priority.

Proof of repair

A worker submits an after-photo, and a different official must verify it before the case can close.

Permanent audit trail

Every action is logged with who did it and when, so there is full accountability.

Live public map

Everyone can see reported issues by risk level, which keeps the work visible.

The life of a report

  1. Report. A citizen chooses the issue type, marks the location, adds a description and uploads a photo. The server creates a case ID such as CVT-KOL-123456.
  2. AI check. The photo is checked to be a real JPEG, PNG or WebP image, then analysed by Gemini. The citizen immediately sees whether it was accepted, flagged for review, and how risky it looks.
  3. Validate. An official reviews the case and validates it, or rejects it if it is invalid.
  4. Assign. An official assigns the case to a field worker from the case page.
  5. Fix. The worker sees the job in the field app, does the repair, and uploads an after-photo with a note. This is a resolution claim.
  6. Verify. An official compares the before and after photos and accepts the repair, or rejects it with a reason. A rejection sends the case back to the worker for rework.
  7. Resolved. Only an accepted verification closes the case. Nothing can skip these steps.

Case status flow

received→validated→assigned→in progress→resolution claimed→resolved

A report can be rejected by an official early on, and a rejected repair returns to in progress for rework.

How the AI risk score works

1. Severity (0–100). Gemini rates the visible danger: size, depth, standing water, exposed hazards, whether it blocks a road, and risk to people and vehicles.

2. Hotspot (0–100). The server counts other open reports of the same kind within 300 metres from the last 60 days. Each one adds 15 points, up to 100.

3. Final risk score = 65% severity + 35% hotspot. Scores of 75+ are critical, 50+ high, 25+ moderate, and the rest low.

If the photo does not match the reported issue, the report is flagged for manual review. If the AI is unavailable, the report is still saved with a default score and an official can re-run the analysis later. The AI only advises: humans make every decision.

Security by design

Closed registration

Only an admin can create staff and worker accounts.

Hashed passwords

Passwords are stored with Argon2 and must be 8+ characters. Repeated wrong logins lock the account temporarily.

Signed sessions

Logins use short-lived signed tokens, and each action checks the user's role on the server.

Server-decided data

Citizens cannot choose their own case ID or risk score. The server does.

Safe uploads

Files are checked by their real contents, limited in size, and renamed. Only the person who just created a report can attach its photo.

Hidden keys

The AI and map keys stay on the server. The browser never sees them.

No skipped steps

The server enforces the status flow, so a case cannot be marked resolved without a verified repair.

Atomic audit log

An action and its audit record are saved together, so nothing happens without a record.
